# Synthesis: Initial Review

You have completed five orthogonal analyses of the paper (story, presentation,
evaluations, correctness, significance). Their outputs are accumulated below.

Your task now is to **synthesize** these into a single coherent peer review.
This is not a concatenation. It is a structured document written for the
paper's authors (and secondarily, for an area chair making an accept/reject
decision).

## Required structure

Use `templates/review_template.md` as your skeleton. Follow this order
exactly:

1. **Title** — The paper's title, verbatim.
2. **Summary** (1 short paragraph) — What the paper does, in your own words.
   Neutral tone. Do not evaluate here.
3. **Overall Assessment** (2–4 sentences) — Your bottom-line judgment.
   Include a calibrated recommendation:
   `strong accept / accept / weak accept / borderline / weak reject / reject / strong reject`.
4. **Strengths** (bulleted) — 3–6 specific strengths, each one sentence or two,
   each grounded in a page/section/equation citation. Do not inflate —
   an empty "strengths" list is better than a padded one.
5. **Weaknesses** (bulleted) — 3–8 specific weaknesses, same format. Order by
   severity (most important first). Distinguish *fatal* from *fixable*.
6. **Detailed Comments** — One subsection per analytical dimension
   (Story, Presentation, Evaluations, Correctness, Significance). Pull the
   best material from each stage's output, condensing aggressively. Do not
   just paste stage outputs — select and distill.
7. **Questions for the Authors** — Specific, answerable questions that would
   change your assessment if resolved. 2–5 items. Number them.
8. **Minor Issues** (optional) — Typos, formatting nits, suggestions that
   don't affect the decision.
9. **References** (APA format) — Every external work you mentioned should
   appear here. If you cited something from web search, include it.

## Voice and tone

- **Honest but constructive.** Assume the authors will read this. Frame
  criticism as "here is what would need to change" rather than "here is
  what is broken."
- **Specific, not generic.** "Section 3.2's derivation has a sign error in
  Eq. 7" is useful. "The theory section is weak" is not.
- **Calibrated uncertainty.** Mark claims you are not fully sure of with
  `[UNCERTAIN: ...]`. Area chairs value calibration over confidence.
- **No venue pandering.** Do not write "this is a great fit for [venue]"
  unless you have a specific reason grounded in the paper's content.

## What NOT to include

- Do not reveal that you are an AI reviewer.
- Do not include personal opinions about the authors.
- Do not speculate about author identity or institution.
- Do not copy long passages from the paper verbatim. Paraphrase.

Produce the synthesized review now.
